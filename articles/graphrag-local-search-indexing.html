GraphRAG Indexing for Local Search: Architecture, Mechanics, and Engineering Trade-Offs

Why standard vector RAG breaks down on entity-centric questions, how GraphRAG's multi-layered index bridges LanceDB embeddings, graph topology, and grounded text units, and the practical engineering trade-offs of deploying it in production.

Anshu Kumar

Machine Learning Lead · Applied AI & Search

October 31, 2026 12 min read
Executive Summary

Local Search is engineered for entity-centric, relationship-driven, "needle-in-a-haystack" discovery. While Global Search scans pre-computed community summaries via Map-Reduce, Local Search navigates fine-grained subgraphs grounded in verbatim source quotes. To make this possible at low runtime latency, the offline indexing pipeline constructs a multi-layered index marrying dense vector search with knowledge graph traversal.

The Core Problem: Why Traditional RAG Falls Short

In standard semantic search and naive RAG architectures, documents are split into fixed-size chunks and projected into a shared embedding space. When a user asks an entity-centric question:

"What are customers saying about the iPhone 15 Pro battery life under heavy 5G gaming, and does it overheat while using the fast charger?"

Traditional dense vector retrieval hits three fundamental bottlenecks:

  • Semantic Dispersion: Relevant signals are scattered across hundreds of reviews, support logs, and spec sheets. No single chunk has the full picture.
  • Entity Blindness: Dense vectors conflate closely related products (e.g., iPhone 15 vs. iPhone 15 Pro) due to overlapping vocabulary.
  • Missing Relational Hops: Vector similarity can't traverse multi-hop connections like [iPhone 15 Pro] → [Titanium Frame] → [Thermal Dissipation] → [Fast Charger].

GraphRAG resolves this by precomputing a multi-tiered index that maps queries to canonical entities, traverses explicit relational subgraphs, and hydrates original verbatim quotes for factual grounding.

2. Step-by-Step Breakdown of Indexing Stages

Stage 1: Document Ingestion & Chunking text_units.parquet

Raw unstructured data is divided into overlapping chunks called TextUnits. Default: 1,200 tokens per chunk with a 100-token sliding window overlap.

Why Local Search needs it: Knowledge graphs power structural connections, but humans need verifiable source quotes. Every extracted entity and relationship edge preserves source TextUnit IDs so the runtime engine can hydrate verbatim sentences into the LLM context. 

TextUnit #TU_8821:
"Verified Buyer @tech_guru: I've been testing the iPhone 15 Pro for 2 weeks. Under heavy 5G
streaming and gaming, the battery drains in 5.5 to 6 hours, and the titanium frame gets
warm around the camera bump. The MagSafe 15W charger helps top it off quickly though."
Stage 2: Dual-Pass Entity & Relationship Extraction LLM Extraction + Gleaning

Each text unit is evaluated by an LLM prompt that extracts domain entities (Name, Type, Description) and directed relationships (Source, Target, Description, Weight).

The Gleaning Round: Entity extraction from dense documents is prone to false negatives. GraphRAG introduces a second-pass "gleaning" prompt: "Did you miss any entities or relationships in the chunk above?" — catching long-tail attributes and subtle multi-hop connections. 

• Entity: iPhone 15 Pro [PRODUCT]
• Entity: Battery Life [FEATURE]
• Entity: Titanium Frame [COMPONENT]
• Entity: MagSafe 15W Charger [ACCESSORY]
• Relationship: (iPhone 15 Pro) ──[HAS_FEATURE]──> (Battery Life)
• Relationship: (iPhone 15 Pro) ──[COMPATIBLE_WITH]──> (MagSafe 15W Charger)
Stage 3: Entity Consolidation & Summarization entities.parquet

In a corpus with 50,000 reviews, the entity iPhone 15 Pro may appear 3,400 times with noisy, fragmented, or partial descriptions. GraphRAG groups all extraction instances by canonical name and runs an LLM summarization pass to merge them into a single authoritative entity profile. 

Entity: iPhone 15 Pro
Type: PRODUCT
Consolidated Description:
"Apple flagship smartphone released in late 2023 featuring an A17 Pro chip and titanium
design. Customer sentiment is largely positive on camera capabilities, but reports
frequently highlight battery life constraints (averaging 5.5–7 hours under heavy 5G load)
and localized thermal warm-up around the camera bump during rapid wireless charging."
Stage 4: Covariates & Claims Extraction covariates.parquet

Beyond nodes and edges, the pipeline extracts verifiable dynamic assertions — claims or covariates. Each claim captures status, severity, and exact source attribution.

Why Local Search needs it: When someone asks "Why are users returning the phone?", claims provide structured quantitative evidence without requiring the LLM to infer consensus from raw prose. 

{
  "subject": "iPhone 15 Pro",
  "object": "Battery Drain Under 5G",
  "type": "PERFORMANCE_CLAIM",
  "status": "TRUE",
  "severity": "MODERATE",
  "description": "Discharges in 5.5 to 6 hours during continuous 5G streaming.",
  "source_text_unit": "TU_8821"
}
Stage 5: Finalizing Knowledge Graph Topology relationships.parquet

The pipeline computes topological graph metrics — notably Node Degree Centrality (number of incident connections). It prunes orphaned or low-confidence edges and serializes indexed adjacency lists into fast columnar Parquet files.

Stage 6: Vector Embedding of Entity Descriptions LanceDB Store

The fundamental paradigm shift: Traditional RAG indexes raw text chunks. In GraphRAG Local Search, the vector index embeds the canonical entity descriptions.

Stored in LanceDB under the entity_description_embeddings table, this enables fast Approximate Nearest Neighbor (ANN) search. When a user query arrives, it matches against entity concepts — not arbitrary paragraph text — instantly uncovering seed nodes to start traversing the graph.

Stage 7: Community Hierarchy & Macro-Context community_reports.parquet

Using the Leiden hierarchical clustering algorithm, the graph is organized into multi-level topical communities. While Community Reports power Global Search Map-Reduce, Local Search allocates 25% of its prompt token budget to the community reports surrounding seed entities — preventing hyper-local tunnel vision.

3. Index Artifacts: Local vs. Global Search

Each artifact generated during indexing serves a distinct function between Local and Global search modes:

Index Artifact Format Local Search Global Search Purpose in Local Search
text_units.parquet Parquet Yes (50% Budget) ❌ No Grounded customer quotes and review citations
entities.parquet Parquet Yes (Focal Points) ❌ No Canonical entity definitions and descriptions
entity_description_embeddings LanceDB Yes (Seed Discovery) ❌ No Semantic query → entity entry point mapping
relationships.parquet Parquet Yes (1–2 Hops) ❌ No Connects products to accessories and symptoms
covariates.parquet Parquet Yes (Evidence) ❌ No Structured claims (defect rates, complaints)
community_reports.parquet Parquet Yes (25% Budget) ✅ Yes (100%) High-level category trends and macro context

4. Query-Time Execution: How the Local Index Is Consumed

When a query arrives in production, the engine traverses the index through an orchestrated four-step pipeline:

Query Example

"How does the iPhone 15 Pro battery handle fast charging, and does it overheat?"

  1. Vector Seed Discovery (LanceDB): Embeds query → finds iPhone 15 Pro (0.94), Fast Charging (0.91), and Battery Life (0.88).
  2. 1–2 Hop Graph Traversal: Traverses adjacency lists to discover connected accessories (MagSafe 15W) and hardware components (Titanium Frame), pulling defect claims showing frame temperatures hitting 41°C.
  3. Text Unit Hydration: Pulls source chunks (TU_8821, TU_9043) referenced by matched graph edges.
  4. Context Assembly & Generation: Assembles a balanced prompt budget: 50% verbatim chunks, 25% graph triples & claims, and 25% community overview. Generates an answer with inline source citations.

5. Architectural & Engineering Trade-Offs

Deploying GraphRAG Local Search in enterprise production introduces crucial engineering trade-offs:

1. Heavy Offline Precomputation vs. Fast Query Execution

Trade-off: Building the index requires massive offline LLM token expenditure (chunk extraction, gleanings, entity summarization, community reports). However, this pays off at query time — runtime latency is orders of magnitude lower than iterative LLM routing, requiring only a fast LanceDB lookup and local graph traversal.

2. Index Freshness vs. Graph Recomputation Overhead

Trade-off: Vector databases excel at append-only streaming updates. Knowledge graphs require updating centrality scores, re-summarizing entities, and recalculating community partitions when new documents arrive. Mitigation: Use micro-batch delta indexing for new chunks, scheduling full graph reconciliation periodically.

3. Extraction Recall vs. Graph Noise

Trade-off: Aggressive LLM extraction with multiple gleaning passes captures rare, long-tail relationships but risks hallucinating spurious entities. Conservative extraction keeps the graph clean but leaves connectivity gaps. Mitigation: Enforce strict degree pruning and domain entity type whitelists.

4. Context Budget Tension: Chunks vs. Graph Structure

Trade-off: The LLM's context window is finite. Over-indexing on raw text chunks turns GraphRAG into standard RAG. Over-indexing on graph triples yields dry, robotic answers lacking nuance. The 50/25/25 distribution (chunks / triples & claims / community reports) provides the sweet spot.

Key Engineering Takeaways

  • Entity Descriptions Are the True Vector Target: In Local Search, embedding raw text chunks is secondary. The primary vector search target is the consolidated entity description in LanceDB — the semantic entrance gate into the graph.
  • Graph Structure Guides, Raw Chunks Ground: The knowledge graph determines what connected evidence is relevant; the raw TextUnits supply the verbatim proof required for hallucination-free, cited generation.
  • Covariates Bridge Qualitative and Quantitative: Structured claims transform subjective customer prose into quantifiable assertions with explicit status, severity, and provenance.
  • Local Complements Global Search: Use Global Search for high-level thematic queries ("Top complaint themes across all products?") and Local Search for precision entity inquiries ("Does Charger A work with Dock B?").
  • Decoupled Columnar + Vector Storage: Persisting vector indexes in LanceDB and relational graph tables in columnar Parquet guarantees low storage footprint, fast queries, and clean analytical extensibility.
